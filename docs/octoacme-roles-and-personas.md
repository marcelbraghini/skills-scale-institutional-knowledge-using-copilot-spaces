# OctoAcme Roles and Personas

This document defines typical roles and responsibilities used in OctoAcme project docs and exercises.
Expanding these role definitions helps teams clarify decision ownership, improve handoffs, and make accountability more explicit as delivery teams and stakeholder groups grow.

---

## Developers

### Role Summary
Developers design, build, test, and deliver software components. They collaborate with product and project leads to implement features that meet acceptance criteria and quality standards.

### Responsibilities
- Implement features and fixes to meet acceptance criteria
- Write and maintain tests and documentation
- Participate in design and code reviews
- Assist in estimating and planning work
- Help identify technical risks and propose mitigations

### Goals
- Deliver reliable, maintainable code
- Reduce cycle time from idea to production
- Maintain high test coverage and observability

### Typical Communication
- Daily standups and sprint planning
- PR descriptions and code review comments
- Technical design docs when needed

### Key Interactions
- Partner with Product Managers to clarify acceptance criteria and trade-offs
- Work with Project Managers and Scrum Masters to surface blockers, estimates, and delivery risks
- Collaborate with UX Designers on implementation details, usability trade-offs, and design feedback
- Confirm business rules and edge cases with Business Analysts before and during delivery

---

## Product Managers

### Role Summary
Product Managers define what should be built to deliver customer and business value. They own the product vision, prioritize the backlog, and measure outcomes.

### Responsibilities
- Define problem statements and success metrics
- Prioritize the roadmap and backlog
- Collaborate with stakeholders and engineering on trade-offs
- Validate solutions through user research and metrics

### Goals
- Maximize customer value and impact
- Make clear, data-driven prioritization decisions
- Ensure product-market fit and usability

### Typical Communication
- Weekly alignment with PM and engineering leads
- Roadmap updates and stakeholder briefings
- Acceptance criteria and feature specs

### Key Interactions
- Align with Project Managers on scope, milestones, dependencies, and stakeholder communications
- Partner with UX Designers on discovery, prototypes, and usability feedback
- Work with Business Analysts to turn business needs into clear requirements and decision-ready options
- Validate priorities and outcomes with stakeholders, especially Executive Sponsors and Customers/Users

---

## Project Managers

### Role Summary
Project Managers coordinate delivery activities, manage schedules, risks, and communications. They enable the team to deliver on commitments efficiently.

### Responsibilities
- Create and maintain project plans and timelines
- Manage risks, dependencies, and resource constraints
- Facilitate meetings (kickoff, planning, retrospectives)
- Ensure consistent project documentation and status reporting
- Coordinate cross-team and stakeholder communication

### Goals
- Deliver projects on time and within scope
- Minimize unplanned work and escalations
- Maintain transparency and alignment across stakeholders

### Typical Communication
- Weekly status updates and stakeholder reports
- Risk registers and decision logs
- Coordination via project boards and meeting facilitation

### Key Interactions
- Partner with Product Managers to balance timeline, scope, and value delivery
- Work with Scrum Masters to maintain team cadence, remove impediments, and improve execution flow
- Coordinate with Business Analysts to track requirement dependencies and unresolved decisions
- Keep stakeholders informed of progress, risks, approvals, and escalation needs

---

## Scrum Masters

### Role Summary
Scrum Masters support the delivery team by facilitating team cadence, removing process blockers, and reinforcing healthy agile ways of working. They help the team improve flow without taking product or project ownership away from other roles.

### Responsibilities
- Facilitate standups, sprint planning, reviews, and retrospectives
- Identify and help remove impediments that slow the team down
- Coach the team on working agreements, estimation, and continuous improvement
- Surface delivery risks and process breakdowns early

### Goals
- Maintain a predictable, sustainable delivery rhythm
- Reduce avoidable blockers and context switching
- Improve team collaboration and continuous improvement

### Typical Communication
- Daily coordination with Developers on blockers and flow
- Regular planning and retrospective facilitation
- Frequent syncs with Project Managers on risks, dependencies, and escalations

### Key Interactions
- Partner with Project Managers on cadence, delivery health, and cross-team dependencies
- Align with Product Managers on backlog readiness and sprint goals
- Support Developers by removing blockers and protecting focus time
- Share recurring delivery patterns and risks that stakeholders should understand through PM updates

## UX Designers

### Role Summary
UX Designers make sure solutions are usable, intuitive, and grounded in customer needs. They bring user research, interaction design, and feedback loops into delivery so teams build the right experience, not just the right feature.

### Responsibilities
- Conduct user research, journey mapping, and usability testing
- Create wireframes, prototypes, and interaction guidance
- Help define user-centered acceptance criteria and success signals
- Incorporate feedback from demos, pilots, and support channels into design updates

### Goals
- Improve product usability and customer satisfaction
- Reduce rework caused by unclear or unvalidated design decisions
- Keep discovery and delivery anchored in real user needs

### Typical Communication
- Discovery workshops with Product Managers and stakeholders
- Design reviews with Developers before and during implementation
- Prototype walkthroughs and usability feedback sessions with Customers/Users

### Key Interactions
- Partner with Product Managers to shape problem statements, priorities, and desired outcomes
- Work with Developers to clarify implementation details and design trade-offs
- Coordinate with Business Analysts to ensure workflows and requirements reflect real user journeys
- Gather feedback from Customers/Users and Internal Stakeholders to refine designs before release

---

## Business Analysts

### Role Summary
Business Analysts translate business goals, processes, and constraints into clear requirements that delivery teams can act on. They reduce ambiguity by connecting stakeholder input, product intent, and implementation detail.

### Responsibilities
- Elicit, document, and refine functional requirements and business rules
- Map current and future-state workflows, dependencies, and decision points
- Support backlog refinement with clear acceptance criteria and edge cases
- Maintain traceability for major decisions, assumptions, and open questions

### Goals
- Reduce requirement churn and late-stage surprises
- Improve shared understanding across business, product, and engineering
- Ensure delivered work matches the intended business process and outcomes

### Typical Communication
- Requirements workshops and clarification sessions with stakeholders
- Backlog refinement with Product Managers and Developers
- Decision and dependency tracking with Project Managers

### Key Interactions
- Work with Product Managers to connect business needs to outcomes and priorities
- Partner with Project Managers to flag decision dependencies and readiness risks
- Support Developers with clarifications on rules, workflows, and edge cases during delivery
- Validate assumptions with stakeholders before changes become costly

## Stakeholders

### Role Summary
Stakeholders provide funding, context, feedback, approvals, and operational constraints that shape delivery. Clarifying stakeholder subtypes improves decision-making by making it clear who gives direction, who provides input, and who approves outcomes.

### Responsibilities
- Provide strategic, operational, customer, or regulatory input
- Review milestones, demos, and decisions at the right time
- Approve funding, priorities, or compliance outcomes when needed
- Raise risks, dependencies, and success criteria that the team must account for

### Goals
- Ensure the project delivers business value, user value, and required controls
- Provide timely decisions so the team can keep moving
- Keep delivery aligned with organizational, customer, and regulatory expectations

### Typical Communication
- Milestone reviews and executive updates
- Discovery interviews, demos, and feedback sessions
- Decision logs, approval requests, and risk escalations

### Stakeholder Subtypes

#### Executive Sponsor
- Owns strategic alignment, funding support, and major go/no-go decisions
- Interacts most often with Project Managers for status and risks, and with Product Managers for value, scope, and expected outcomes

#### Customer/User
- Provides direct feedback on pain points, workflows, and usability
- Interacts most often with Product Managers and UX Designers during discovery, prototypes, demos, and validation

#### Regulatory/Compliance
- Ensures legal, security, privacy, or policy requirements are accounted for early enough to avoid rework
- Interacts most often with Business Analysts, Project Managers, and Developers at requirement review, release readiness, and risk escalation points

#### Internal Stakeholder
- Represents adjacent teams such as support, operations, sales, enablement, or leadership functions that depend on the project outcome
- Interacts most often with Project Managers on planning and rollout readiness, and with Product Managers on impact and change communication

### Key Interactions
- Executive Sponsors align with Project Managers and Product Managers on priorities, funding, and escalations
- Customers/Users collaborate with Product Managers and UX Designers to validate whether solutions solve real problems
- Regulatory/Compliance partners with Business Analysts, Project Managers, and Developers to reduce approval surprises late in delivery
- Internal Stakeholders coordinate with Project Managers and Product Managers on rollout impacts, training needs, and adoption risks

## Collaboration and handoff guide

| Process moment | Primary owner | Key contributors | Expected handoff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Discovery and problem framing | Product Managers | UX Designers, Business Analysts, Customers/Users | Problem statement, user needs, and success metrics are documented before planning |
| Backlog readiness and planning | Project Managers | Product Managers, Scrum Masters, Business Analysts, Developers | Prioritized work, dependencies, and acceptance criteria are ready for execution |
| Delivery execution and blocker removal | Developers | Scrum Masters, Project Managers, UX Designers, Business Analysts | Questions, blockers, and design gaps are resolved quickly with clear owners |
| Decision and approval checkpoints | Stakeholders | Project Managers, Product Managers, Business Analysts | Decisions are logged with owners, rationale, and next steps |
| Release readiness and rollout communication | Project Managers | Developers, Internal Stakeholders, Regulatory/Compliance | Launch risks, approvals, and change impacts are visible before release |

## Why these additions improve project outcomes
- They reduce ambiguity by separating delivery coordination, backlog ownership, design, and requirements work into clearly named responsibilities.
- They improve handoffs by showing which roles must collaborate before work moves from discovery to planning, execution, and release.
- They increase accountability by making stakeholder decision points and approval responsibilities more explicit.
- They help new team members understand where to go for decisions, clarification, feedback, and escalation.
